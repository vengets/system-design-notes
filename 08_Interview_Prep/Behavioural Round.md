### Exit Statement:

My role ended as part of a broader organisational restructure that affected multiple teams within the company.

I’m genuinely excited about the timing because it gives me the opportunity to explore roles with companies like yours, which is something I’ve been wanting to do for a while.

**
### Story 1: Zilingo Partial Order Cancellation Feature

“At Zilingo, we only supported full order cancellation. One day I learned from customer support that a family in Indonesia couldn’t exchange just one dress for their daughter before a festival because they would have needed to return the entire order.

That made me realize the customer impact of the missing feature, so I took ownership of implementing partial order cancellation. The change required refactoring parts of the order and refund flow, but I completed the implementation over a weekend, got it tested, fixed a QA issue, and deployed it safely during low-traffic hours.

The feature went live successfully, helped customers immediately, and the CTO appreciated the initiative. It taught me the importance of connecting engineering work directly to customer experience.”

  

### Story 2: Frontend Linting / CI Cleanup Feedback Story

“At one point, I noticed some frontend linting issues had been sitting unresolved for a long time because the CI pipeline wasn’t enforcing them properly. Since I had some bandwidth, I fixed the issues myself and raised a pull request.

During review, I received feedback that although the cleanup was useful, it wasn’t aligned with the sprint plan and I should have discussed it with the manager beforehand. That helped me realize that as engineers become more senior, alignment and visibility are just as important as technical improvements.

Since then, I’ve become more proactive about communicating improvement work early and ensuring it fits team priorities before execution.”

  

### Story 3: Mentoring / Helping Junior Engineers 

At Samsung, one of the junior engineers on the team was struggling to contribute to a microservices feature because they were having difficulty even setting up the local development environment.

I sat with them and did a pair-programming session where I explained the overall system architecture, which services needed to run locally, how to test endpoints, and some productivity workflows I personally used. For example, I showed how to use iTerm split panes to monitor logs from multiple services together and how to use Node.js watch mode to automatically rebuild and restart services during development.

Together, we implemented and tested one endpoint using Postman. After that session, I noticed the engineer becoming much more confident and independently using the same workflow and tools going forward.

What I liked most about that experience was that instead of just solving the immediate task, I was able to help someone become more productive and self-sufficient long term.
